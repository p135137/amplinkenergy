The sites where peaks pile up.

Why battery storage.

A commercial electric bill is set by a few expensive minutes. A battery can cover them, and can earn money the rest of the year.

Cut demand charges

A battery supplies power during your highest-demand minutes, which can lower the peak your utility bills you for.

Get paid for flexibility

Batteries can be enrolled in demand response programs that pay for reducing grid use at set times.

Keep critical loads running

Some systems can also supply backup power during an outage.

Questions we hear most.

What is a demand charge?

A fee based on the highest amount of power (kW) you draw in a short interval during the billing period. Many commercial and industrial rates include one, and it can be a large part of the bill.

What is the difference between kW and kWh, and why does it matter for sizing?

kW is power: how fast a battery can discharge. kWh is energy: how long it can keep going. A demand charge is set by kW, so a battery that shaves a peak needs enough kW to cover the spike and enough kWh to hold it for the length of the peak. Most commercial systems are built for about two to four hours of discharge at rated power.

How exactly does a battery lower a demand charge?

The battery charges when your load is low and discharges when your load nears its peak, so the meter sees a lower number. The savings depend on how reliably it catches the peak. The utility bills on the single highest interval in the month, often 15 minutes, so one missed peak can erase a month of savings. Some tariffs also set a ratchet that carries a high peak into later months, which makes catching peaks even more important.

What kind of batteries are used, and are they safe?

Most commercial and industrial systems today use lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells, which are valued for cycle life and thermal stability. In the U.S., a complete system is typically certified to UL 9540, tested for fire spread under UL 9540A, and installed to NFPA 855 and local fire code. Your local fire marshal and utility review the installation, and the provider handles those approvals.

Can the same battery do demand response and peak shaving?

Often yes, but the two can compete. A demand response event may call for the battery at a time when you would rather hold it for your own peak, and the battery cannot be in two states at once. Providers handle this by reserving part of the capacity or by scheduling around event windows. Whether a program allows it, and how much it pays, depends on your utility and grid operator, so we ask every provider how they would handle it.

Do you install batteries?

No. The battery provider, a company that installs and operates batteries, handles engineering, installation, contracts and enrollment. We check sites, run the bidding for facilities and, for battery companies, run co-marketing and referral work through their channel partner programs.
